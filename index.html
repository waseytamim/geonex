CLASS 01 · GIS FOUNDATIONS

What is Geographic Information System (GIS)?

GIS is a framework for collecting, managing, analysing and visualising data that is connected to locations on Earth.

Learning objectives

  • Explain GIS in your own words.
  • Identify the five core components of GIS.
  • Distinguish spatial and non-spatial data.
  • Understand the difference between vector and raster data.

The five components of GIS

01 · HardwareComputers, GPS devices and servers.
02 · SoftwareQGIS, ArcGIS and related tools.
03 · DataMaps, satellite imagery and attributes.
04 · PeopleAnalysts, researchers and decision-makers.
05 · MethodsStandards and workflows for analysis.

Key concepts

Spatial data describes where something is located, such as a river line or soil sampling point. Non-spatial data describes its properties, such as soil pH, sample ID or land-use category.

Vector data represents features as points, lines and polygons. Raster data represents information as a grid of cells, such as satellite imagery or elevation.

Remember: GIS is not just digital mapping. It connects location-based data with analysis to help answer real-world questions.

Your first practical task

  1. Install QGIS when you are ready.
  2. Create a folder named GeoNexa_Projects.
  3. Write one example of GIS use in soil or environmental science.
